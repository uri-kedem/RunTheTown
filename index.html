Terms & policy

What RunTheTown does with your data and what you agree to by using it. The full privacy policy and support page: privacy policy · support.

Run at your own risk

Routes are drawn automatically from OpenStreetMap data, which can be wrong or out of date. A path may be closed, unlit, unsafe or private, and a track or stadium may not be open to the public. Check the route makes sense before you set off, obey traffic rules and signals, and use your own judgement at all times. Voice guidance is a convenience, not a substitute for looking where you are going. You use RunTheTown entirely at your own risk; it comes with no warranty of any kind.

Your location

Your position is used on your device to set the start point, to follow you along the route and to find running spots around you. It is never sent to a RunTheTown server (there is none) and never stored by us. To fetch map data and look up places, the app sends coordinates to the public services below; they receive the area you are looking at, not your identity.

Your profile and your runs stay on the device

Your name, e-mail address and photo, and every run in your journal (distance, time, the route, the GPS track and a picture of it) are stored only on this device. Nothing is sent to us: there is no server and no account. Delete a run from its page, export them all as a file, or delete the app to remove everything. Downloaded map pieces, elevation data and place details are cached for up to 14 days so a city loads faster the next time.

No tracking

No analytics, no advertising and no tracking of any kind. "Report a problem" opens your own e-mail app with a message to us; it is only sent if you press send.

Services used

  • OpenFreeMap for the street and park data, Esri and OpenStreetMap for the background map pictures.
  • Microsoft Planetary Computer (ESA WorldCover) for land cover, AWS Terrain Tiles and Open-Meteo for elevation.
  • Overpass API for traffic lights and crossings (best effort).
  • Photon (komoot) and Nominatim for address search and for finding tracks, stadiums and parks.
  • Wikidata and Wikipedia for a place's website and article.

Each of these has its own terms and privacy policy. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License.

Fair use

These are free public services with usage limits. Please use the app as a runner would, not for bulk downloads or automated queries.

Changes

Install on your phone

You are running the installed app. Nothing more to do.

RunTheTown installs straight from the browser, with no app store: it gets its own icon on your home screen, opens full screen, keeps the map pieces you downloaded, and voice guidance and GPS work as in any app.

iPhone and iPad (Safari)

  1. Open this page in Safari (other browsers on iPhone cannot install apps).
  2. Tap the Share button at the bottom of the screen.
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.

Keep the screen on while you run: an installed web app on iPhone stops receiving GPS when the phone is locked. The app asks the phone to keep the screen awake during a run.

Android (Chrome)

  1. Tap the ⋮ menu at the top right of Chrome.
  2. Tap Add to Home screen (or Install app), then Install.

On a computer

Chrome and Edge show an install icon at the right end of the address bar; the app then opens in its own window.
