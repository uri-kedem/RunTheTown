Terms & policy

What RunTheTown does with your data and what you agree to by using it. The full privacy policy and support page: privacy policy · support.

Run at your own risk

Routes are drawn automatically from OpenStreetMap data, which can be wrong or out of date. A path may be closed, unlit, unsafe or private, and a track or stadium may not be open to the public. Check the route makes sense before you set off, obey traffic rules and signals, and use your own judgement at all times. Voice guidance is a convenience, not a substitute for looking where you are going. You use RunTheTown entirely at your own risk; it comes with no warranty of any kind.

Your location

Your position is used on your device to set the start point, to follow you along the route and to find running spots around you. While you run it is not sent to us. As a guest, nothing about where you run is stored by us; if you have signed in, the runs you save, with their GPS tracks, are kept in your account (see below). To fetch map data and look up places, the app sends the area you are looking at, and what you type in the search box, to the services below. Search, place lookups and the request for traffic lights go through RunTheTown's own relay, a small server on Cloudflare that passes them on to the public services and keeps no record of who asked.

Your profile and your runs

Signing in is optional, and the app works fully without it. As a guest, your name and photo, and every run in your journal (distance, time, the route, the GPS track and a picture of it) are stored only on this device, and none of it is sent to us. In the iPhone app a run you keep is also saved to Apple Health when you allow it, and shown on your Apple Watch when you use one. Delete a run from its page, export them all as a file, or delete the app to remove everything. Downloaded map pieces, elevation data and place details are cached for up to 14 days so a city loads faster the next time.

If you sign in

You can sign in with Apple, with Google, or with a code sent to your e-mail address; there is no password. Your account then keeps, on RunTheTown's own server at Cloudflare: your e-mail address, the name and photo of your profile, and the runs in your journal (distance, time, the route and the GPS track, without the map picture). It is kept so that your runs are backed up and appear on your other devices, and for nothing else: it is not shared, not sold and not used for advertising. When you sign in with Apple or Google we receive from them your e-mail address, your name and their identifier for you, and never your password. Settings and routes downloaded for later stay on the device. "Delete my account" on the profile page removes the account and everything in it from the server at once. Signing out leaves the account as it is and leaves your runs on the device.

No tracking

No analytics, no advertising and no tracking of any kind. "Report a problem" opens your own e-mail app with a message to us; it is only sent if you press send.

Services used

  • OpenFreeMap for the map and for the street and park data.
  • Microsoft Planetary Computer (ESA WorldCover) for land cover, AWS Terrain Tiles and Open-Meteo for elevation.
  • Overpass API for traffic lights and crossings, and after dark for the ways without street lights (best effort).
  • Photon (komoot) and Nominatim for address search and for finding tracks, stadiums and parks.
  • Wikidata and Wikipedia for a place's website and article, and for the stories on an Explorer tour.
  • Cloudflare runs RunTheTown's relay, which stands between the app and Photon, Nominatim and Overpass. It keeps their answers for up to 30 days so the same question is not asked twice, and not who asked. Like any web server it handles your internet address to send the answer back.
  • Apple and Google confirm who you are when you sign in with them, and Resend delivers the sign-in code when you sign in by e-mail. They are used only if you sign in.

Each of these has its own terms and privacy policy. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License.

Fair use

Most of these are free public services with usage limits. Please use the app as a runner would, not for bulk downloads or automated queries.

Install on your phone

You are running the installed app. Nothing more to do.

RunTheTown installs straight from the browser, with no app store: it gets its own icon on your home screen, opens full screen, keeps the map pieces you downloaded, and voice guidance and GPS work as in any app.

iPhone and iPad (Safari)

  1. Open this page in Safari (other browsers on iPhone cannot install apps).
  2. Tap the Share button at the bottom of the screen.
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.

Keep the screen on while you run: an installed web app on iPhone stops receiving GPS when the phone is locked. The app asks the phone to keep the screen awake during a run.

Android (Chrome)

  1. Tap the ⋮ menu at the top right of Chrome.
  2. Tap Add to Home screen (or Install app), then Install.

On a computer

Chrome and Edge show an install icon at the right end of the address bar; the app then opens in its own window.
